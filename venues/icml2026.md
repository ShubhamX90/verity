# ICML 2026 requirements

Verified against the live pages on 2026-09-03. Re-verify against these sources directly before relying on anything here for a final submission decision — policy pages can be revised after a CFP goes out. Note this cycle's submission/review process has already fully concluded as of the verification date, so these pages describe a completed cycle; ICML typically reuses the same style file and most policy structure year over year, but re-check the live next-year CFP once it posts:

- https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/PeerReviewFAQ
- https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/LLM-Policy

## Page limits

- **Submission**: main paper body ≤ 8 pages. References, the Impact Statement, and appendices are unlimited and don't count toward this. AuthorInstructions: *"up to eight pages long, followed by any number of pages for references and appendices."* Max submission file size is 50MB.
- **Camera-ready**: main body ≤ 9 pages (one extra page granted for acceptance), *"followed by any acknowledgements, the impact statement (for main track papers), references, and appendices."* Max camera-ready file size is 20MB.
- Formatting must use the official style files; US letter size (not A4) is required. LaTeX is the only supported format — "There is no support for any typesetting software other than LaTeX."

## Anonymization (double-blind)

- *"Reviewing for ICML 2026 is double-blind: reviewers will not know the authors' identities and vice versa."*
- Refer to your own prior work in the third person; exclude acknowledgments and grant/funding numbers from the submitted version; avoid linking to non-anonymous public code repositories; preserve anonymity in author responses during rebuttal too.
- The author list is locked at the abstract deadline: *"The author list cannot be changed after the abstract deadline."* After that point authors may be reordered, but any addition or removal "must be justified in writing and approved on a case-by-case basis by the program chairs."
- On camera-ready: author order may be changed, but no author additions or deletions are permitted at that stage.

## AI / generative-AI use — two distinct policies (authors vs. reviewers)

**For authors (writing/research assistance)** — permissive, responsibility-based rather than a disclosure form:
- *"Authors are allowed to use generative AI tools such as Large Language Models (LLMs) to assist in writing or research."*
- *"Authors must take full responsibility for all content in their paper, including any content generated by AI tools that might be construed as plagiarism or scientific misconduct."*
- *"LLMs are not eligible for authorship."*
- No structured required/recommended disclosure taxonomy like ICLR 2027's — the CallForPapers text does not ask for an itemized AI-use statement from authors on the writing side. Don't invent one.
- **Prompt injection is explicitly and separately banned**: *"Any attempts at prompt injection are strictly forbidden and will result in desk rejection."* (This is about embedding hidden instructions targeting AI reviewers/tools, not about disclosure — keep it distinct from the authorship-assistance policy above.)

**For reviewers (a separate policy that affects authors indirectly)** — the `LLM-Policy` page describes two reviewer tracks that *authors themselves select* when submitting:
- **Policy A** (conservative): LLM use in any stage of reviewing is strictly prohibited, aside from inadvertent use of standard tools like spell-checkers.
- **Policy B** (permissive): reviewers may feed submissions into privacy-compliant LLMs to help understand the paper and polish review text, but may not delegate judgment — *"ask LLMs to assess paper's quality/significance"* is disallowed.
- Each paper's chosen policy (A or B) is visible only to that paper's own authors and is not published in the proceedings.
- Reviewers are fully responsible for their review content; hallucinated review content is subject to disciplinary action. A reviewer who deviates from their assigned policy commits an integrity violation that "may result in actions by the program chairs (including the desk rejection of their own submissions)" — i.e., a violating reviewer's *own* papers as an author can be desk rejected, not the paper they reviewed.

## Reviewing obligations (reciprocal reviewing)

Two independent triggers for mandatory reviewing, per the PeerReviewFAQ:

- **Per-submission**: a submission needs an author designated as its reciprocal reviewer, unless that submission is marked exempt.
- **Per-author**: any author on **4 or more full submissions** (as of the full-submission deadline) must serve as a reviewer, unless they file the Per-author Reciprocal Reviewing Exemption form.
- Exemptions exist for: papers withdrawn or desk rejected before the full-submission deadline; authors under the 4-submission threshold (when not separately listed as a reciprocal reviewer); and authors at/above the 4-submission threshold who filed the exemption form.
- Organizing-committee roles (ACs, SACs) are excluded from the author-side quota.
- Non-compliance is treated as a real desk-rejection risk, not a soft ask — see below.

## Ethics / impact statement (required)

- An **Impact Statement is mandatory** for main-track papers (not required for the position-paper track): *"Authors are required to include a statement of the potential broader impact of their work, including its ethical aspects and future societal consequences."*
- Placed in an unnumbered section at the end of the paper, co-located with Acknowledgments (either order, but both before References); does not count toward the page limit.
- A boilerplate default is explicitly sanctioned when there's nothing venue-specific to add: *"This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of Machine Learning. There are many potential societal consequences of our work, none which we feel must be specifically highlighted here."* — usable verbatim, though ICML "encourages" more specific discussion where relevant.
- A separate **Conflict-of-Interest disclosure** paragraph is required in the introduction for financial conflicts (new addition per the fetched AuthorInstructions — confirm current wording against the live page, since this is the kind of requirement that gets refined year to year).
- A submission-side ethics review process exists: reviewers/ACs may flag ethical concerns; an ethics committee provides comments but cannot independently reject a paper.

## Reproducibility

- No mandatory checklist (unlike NeurIPS). Authors are "encouraged to submit code," and reproducibility/code-availability is explicitly a factor in decision-making: *"Reproducibility of results and easy availability of code will be taken into account in the decision-making process."* Treat this as an incentive, not a hard gate.

## Desk-rejection triggers

- Main body over the 8-page submission limit — the AuthorInstructions state this in absolute terms: *"any submission whose main body goes over the 8 page limit will be automatically rejected."*
- Any failure to anonymize or to follow the required format: *"All submissions must be anonymized and follow the required format; otherwise, they will automatically be rejected."*
- Prompt injection attempts, in either direction (targeting AI reviewing tools).
- Non-compliance with the per-submission or per-author reciprocal-reviewing requirement.
- A reviewer's own submissions can be desk rejected if that reviewer violates their assigned (A/B) reviewing-LLM policy — an indirect trigger worth knowing if any author on your paper is also reviewing.
- Dual submission: identical or substantially similar work already published, accepted, or under parallel submission elsewhere — organizers reserve the right to reject or remove such submissions from the proceedings.
