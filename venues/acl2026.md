## The shared ARR mechanism (also applies to EMNLP and NAACL — see `venues/emnlp2026.md` and `venues/naacl2027.md`)

ACL, EMNLP, and NAACL (plus EACL, COLING, IJCNLP-AACL) do not run independent peer review. Papers are submitted to a rolling **ACL Rolling Review (ARR)** cycle, reviewed there, and then **committed** to a specific conference's cycle after reviews and a meta-review exist. Page limits, anonymization rules, the mandatory Limitations section, and the Responsible NLP Research Checklist (including its AI-disclosure question) are set by ARR itself and are identical across the whole family for a given cycle — they are **not** re-decided by each conference. What each conference (ACL, EMNLP, NAACL) *does* set independently: which ARR cycle(s) feed it, its own reviewer-registration/commitment deadlines, and a handful of its own added desk-rejection notes (see below). This profile states the shared ARR rules in full rather than pointing elsewhere, because this skill loads one venue-year file per session — but where a rule is genuinely ARR-wide rather than ACL-specific, that's noted so you don't mistake it for something unique to ACL.

## Page limits

- **Long papers**: up to 8 pages of content. References are unlimited and don't count. The mandatory Limitations section (below) also doesn't count and goes after the content, before references.
- **Short papers**: up to 4 pages of content, same references/Limitations carve-out.
- **Camera-ready**: accepted long papers get one additional content page (up to 9); accepted short papers get one additional content page (up to 5) — "so that reviewers' comments can be taken into account."
- Appendices are unlimited in length but must be in the same double-column format as the main paper; reviewers are not obligated to read them, and moving required main-text content into an appendix to dodge the limit is explicitly called out as a submission problem.

## Anonymization (double-blind)

- Standard ARR double-blind policy: no author names, affiliations, or identifying self-references in the main text or supplementary material. Self-citation must be phrased in third person ("Smith et al. showed…", not "In our prior work…").
- **No anonymity period**: as of February 15, 2024, ARR policy allows posting or discussing non-anonymous preprints of a submission at any time while it's under review — this is a real policy change from older ACL-family norms, don't assume the old preprint embargo still applies.
- Supplementary material must also be anonymized. Cloud-storage links that could deanonymize via download tracking (e.g. a personal Dropbox) are prohibited; links to non-anonymous code repositories are a listed desk-rejection-adjacent problem.
- Confidential comments to Area Chairs/Senior Area Chairs must not contain author-identifying information either — authors stay anonymous to ACs/SACs, not just to reviewers.

## AI use disclosure

There is no separate "AI use statement" section the way ICLR has one. Disclosure instead lives inside the mandatory **Responsible NLP Research Checklist** (submitted as part of the submission form, not as prose in the paper), which has had an AI-assistance question since the ACL 2023 policy:

- **Checklist question E1**: "If you used any AI assistants, did you include information about your use?" Use must conform to the ACL Publication Ethics Policy, especially its authorship criteria (an AI system cannot be an author).
- If AI writing/research assistance was used, authors must elaborate on the scope and nature of that use — the policy's own framing is that this question is "not meant for automatic desk-rejections, but rather for author reflection and establishment of research norms in the field." Checklist answers are visible to reviewers, who can flag a paper for case-by-case ethics review on that basis.
- Appropriate/expected uses (per the CFP's own examples): language polishing, predictive-keyboard-style assistance, literature search, low-novelty boilerplate generation. Separately and unconditionally desk-rejectable regardless of disclosure: using AI to manipulate "machine reader" behavior (prompt injection against automated screening/review tools) in the submission.
- **Practical implication for using this skill**: several of this skill's own workflows (prose editing, citation formatting, structure suggestions, compression) fall under "AI assistance" for checklist purposes. Keep a running note of what the skill did and on which sections, the same way the ICLR profile recommends — it's the honest source for filling in checklist question E1 (and the Limitations section's own AI-use caveats, if any) rather than reconstructing it after the fact.

## Reviewing obligations

- Since May 2025, **all submitting authors are expected to sign up as reviewers** for the ARR cycle they submit to (not just one designated author per paper, unlike ICLR's "one registered reviewer per submission" model). Registration is due within 48 hours of that cycle's submission deadline — check the live ARR cycle page for the exact date, this profile doesn't track a calendar.
- Authors who are "highly irresponsible" as reviewers (missed/low-quality reviews) risk becoming ineligible to (re)submit or commit a paper during the current and the next ARR cycle.
- ACL 2026 specifically also required a presenting author to be registered by ACL's own early-registration deadline; papers without one are subject to desk rejection (see below) — this is an ACL-added condition on top of the shared ARR reviewing rule.
- Organizing-committee roles are exempt in the usual way, per general ARR practice.

## Reproducibility / ethics — the Limitations section

- Unlike ICLR's optional reproducibility statement, the ACL family's **Limitations section is mandatory**, not recommended. It goes after the main content, before references, and does not count toward the page limit. It should address genuine methodological caveats and confounding factors affecting the results — the guidance explicitly discourages using it as a disguised "future work" section.
- A separate, situational **ethics considerations** discussion is optional and, if included, doesn't count toward the limit either — used when the paper raises Code-of-Ethics-relevant questions (human subjects, sensitive data, dual use, etc.). Flagged papers go to the ARR/ACL Ethics Advisory Committee for review.
- The Responsible NLP Research Checklist itself is mandatory and covers five sections beyond AI use: (A) limitations/risks for every submission, (B) scientific artifacts — licensing, intended use, anonymization of released data — (C) computational-experiment reporting (parameters, compute budget, hyperparameters, result variance), (D) human-subjects/annotator details (recruitment, consent, IRB, demographics), and (E) AI assistant use. Systematically skipping relevant sections or failing to justify a "No" is itself a desk-rejection ground.

## Desk-rejection triggers

- Exceeding the page limit (8/4 content pages, or moving required content into an appendix to evade it).
- Any anonymity violation: revealed identity, non-anonymized supplementary material, identifying links, or identity in confidential AC/SAC comments.
- Dual/parallel submission to another archival venue while under ARR review; undisclosed "salami-sliced" resubmission of prior low-scoring work without meaningful revision; multiple near-duplicate copies by the same authors.
- Missing or misplaced Limitations section; a Responsible NLP Research Checklist that is incomplete, generic ("yes" with no justification), or misleading.
- Not following the official ACL style template, or a submission with leftover reviewer comments / meta-text from a prior revision cycle.
- Prompt-injection or other attempts to manipulate automated "machine readers" of the submission.
- Placeholder/duplicate abstracts, or a paper outside ARR's stated CFP scope.
- ACL-2026-specific: no presenting author registered by ACL's own early-registration deadline.
- Desk rejection can happen at any point in the review cycle, including after initial reviews are already in — it isn't limited to an automated pre-check at submission time.

## Template

`templates/acl-style-files/` — the official *ACL family LaTeX/Word style-file package, fetched directly from `github.com/acl-org/acl-style-files` (master branch) on 2026-09-03. Contains `acl_latex.tex` (main LaTeX/pdfLaTeX entry point), `acl_lualatex.tex` (XeLaTeX/LuaLaTeX variant), `acl.sty`, `acl_natbib.bst`, `anthology.bib.txt` and `custom.bib` (bibliography resources), `formatting.md`, and `README.md`. This package is shared verbatim across ACL, EMNLP, and NAACL — copy the whole directory when starting a new paper; don't edit the `.sty`/`.bst` files directly.
