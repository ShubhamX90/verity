# ICLR 2027 requirements

Verified against the live pages on 2026-09-03. Re-verify against these sources directly before relying on anything here for a final submission decision — policy pages can be revised after a CFP goes out:
- https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers
- https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines
- https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors

## Page limits

- **Submission**: main text ≤ 9 pages. References do not count. Appendices are unlimited but reviewers are not required to read them.
- **Camera-ready**: 10 pages (identical to the submission-version limit stated in the template; the Author Guidelines describe this as "identical with the submission version (10 pages)" — read as: 10 pages is the operative camera-ready number, treat the template's own in-body "10 pages for rebuttal/camera ready" line as the authoritative phrasing).
- The AI use statement, ethics statement, and reproducibility statement (below) do **not** count toward this limit.

## Anonymization (double-blind)

- All submitted papers must be anonymous in both main text and supplementary material. **Any revealed author identity anywhere is a desk-rejection**, not a warning.
- Self-citation is allowed without breaking anonymity, but must be phrased in third person ("Smith et al. showed..." not "In our prior work...").
- Code sharing: either (1) an anonymized `.zip` submitted as supplementary material, or (2) a link to an anonymous repository. No de-anonymizing metadata (contributor names, non-anonymous commit history, identifying paths).
- Any external link or demo must be completely anonymous and must not track visitors (no analytics that could deanonymize via referrer/account).

This is what `pre-submission-checklist.md` / `readiness_check.sh` runs mechanically when this profile is active (author-field content, self-citation phrasing, non-anonymous URLs, acknowledgments section) — a **Tier A** check (read-only, reports candidates for you to confirm), never an autonomous "fix."

## AI use disclosure — mandatory, tightened for 2027

This is **required**, not conditional — every submission includes it, even if the honest content is "we did not use generative AI for any required-disclosure task." It does not count toward the page limit.

**No page cap in the actual policy**, despite the bundled template's own boilerplate suggesting one. The live AI Policy for Authors page states: *"this section will not be counted towards the page limit... we encourage you to provide as much detail as you want!"* The bundled template's boilerplate text (`templates/iclr2027/iclr2027_conference.tex:411-412`) says "This statement should not be more than 1 page" — that's the template's own suggested default, not conference policy; don't truncate an honest, complete AI disclosure to fit it.

**Required disclosure** (must be reported if used):
synthetic data generation; developing theoretical models or conceptual frameworks; formulating mathematical claims; providing critical ingredients for proving mathematical claims; assisting with proof writing; proposing or refining hypotheses; designing or providing feedback on research methodology or experiments; implementing methods; assisting with translation; cleaning and reformatting datasets; supporting qualitative and thematic data analysis; interpreting results.

**Recommended disclosure** (should be reported if used):
formulating survey or interview questions; creating or modifying scientific figures or images; suggesting experimental parameters; creating or editing software code; drafting parts of a research paper; transcribing recordings; summarizing or analyzing existing literature; discovering research topics or identifying gaps; brainstorming; searching for information; editing papers for readability; identifying relevant literature; formatting references; suggesting paper structure, titles, or keywords; creation of artifacts.

**Consequence for non-disclosure**: "A substantial falsehood, instance of plagiarism, or misrepresentation produced by an LLM would be considered a Code of Ethics violation on the part of the paper's authors, and might lead to desk rejection."

**Practical implication for using this skill**: several of this skill's own workflows fall directly into the *recommended*-disclosure list (drafting/rewording prose, editing for readability, formatting references/citations, suggesting structure) and, if the compression toolkit or citation-verification workflow ever touches methodology framing, potentially the *required*-disclosure list too. Keep a running note of what this skill actually did (which workflows, on which sections) as you go — it's a better source for an accurate AI use statement than reconstructing it from memory later. This doesn't need tooling; a plain running list in your own notes is enough.

## Reviewing obligations

- **All submissions must have at least one author registered to review at least 3 papers.**
- Eligibility to serve as the registered reviewer requires at least one accepted publication (primary conference/journal paper only — not workshop papers, tiny papers, blog posts, demos, or industry papers) at: ICLR, NeurIPS, ICML, UAI, AISTATS, JMLR, TMLR (incl. Datasets & Benchmarks), ACL, EMNLP, EACL, NAACL, IJCNLP-AACL, CL, TACL (incl. Findings), COLM, CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, PAMI, 3DV (incl. Findings), AAAI, IJCAI, JAIR, ICRA, IROS, RSS, CoRL, KDD, COLT.
- Eligibility to serve as the registered reviewer is fixed as of the abstract-submission cutoff for this cycle — a publication accepted after that point doesn't count. Check the current CFP for the exact date.
- Authors who are co-authors on 3+ ICLR 2027 submissions must review at least 6 papers; failure risks desk rejection.
- Authors exempt from the eligibility rule (no qualifying publication, and none of their co-authors have one either) may submit at most one paper.
- Organizing-committee members (AC/SAC/other chairs) are exempt from the reviewing requirement.

This is a submission-logistics requirement, not something this skill can verify or enforce — it's listed here so it isn't missed, not because there's a mechanical check for it.

## Reproducibility statement (recommended)

A paragraph-long statement (does not count toward the page limit) pointing to whatever in the paper, appendix, or supplementary material supports reproducibility — code availability, proof completeness, dataset documentation, hyperparameter/seed disclosure. See `reproducibility.md` for the mechanical audit this skill can run to help populate this statement accurately (seeds, versions, git state, data hashes) rather than writing it from impression.

## Ethics statement (recommended, situational)

Optional; include only if the paper raises Code-of-Ethics-relevant questions (human subjects, sensitive data/bias, dual-use concerns, conflicts of interest, IRB/compliance). ≤1 page, doesn't count toward the limit.

## Desk-rejection triggers

- Main text exceeds the page limit.
- Author identity revealed anywhere (main text or supplementary).
- A co-author's OpenReview profile has incorrect information. A *missing* profile as of the abstract-submission cutoff is explicitly **not** a desk-rejection trigger on its own — the Author Guidelines FAQ states there's a grace period through the full-paper deadline: *"As long as ALL authors have OR account before the full paper deadline, the paper will not be desk-rejected."* Only incorrect/wrong profile info is an immediate trigger; a missing one has until the full-paper submission point.
- No eligible author registered to review 3+ papers (unless exempt).
- Failing the reciprocal-reviewing obligation (for authors on 3+ submissions).
- Placeholder or duplicate abstract.
- Sanctioned-entity affiliation.
- Co-authorship quota: no author may appear as a co-author on more than 20 papers; submissions in excess of this (or a related per-submission quota) are subject to random desk rejection among the excess.

The OpenReview-profile, reciprocal-reviewing, sanctioned-entity, and co-authorship-quota items are account/logistics matters outside what a repo-scoped skill can verify — listed here so they aren't forgotten, not because this skill checks them. `pre-submission-checklist.md` covers what's mechanically checkable from this list (page count, anonymization signals).
